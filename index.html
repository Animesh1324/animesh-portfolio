Approach

Check the evidence before making the claim

I trained as a pharmacist before I trained as a manager, and both shape how I work. Laboratory training taught me that a result means little until the method behind it holds up. The MBA taught me that a good plan still has to survive the price, the prescriber and the regulator.

In practice, I let field data change the plan when it disagrees with me. I use a trial only for the population it actually enrolled, and I state sample limits instead of hiding them. I am early in my career, and I would rather show exactly what I have done than suggest more.

Case 01Internship projectMicro Labs Ltd. · May–Jul 2026

GLUMIC: launching semaglutide as a late entrant

  1. Challenge

    India's GLP-1 class reached ₹1,906 Cr (PharmaTrac, MAT May 2026) after semaglutide's compound patent expired on 20 March 2026. More than 35 semaglutide brands from 17 companies were already competing, and tirzepatide held 63% of class value. The brief was to plan a launch for a company with no GLP-1 heritage.

  2. Evidence

    I ran two instruments in three Jaipur micro-markets. The first was an RCPA of 40 pharmacies, scored on quality, prescriber linkage and potential. The second was a signed questionnaire completed by 10 specialists in endocrinology, diabetology, internal medicine and gastroenterology. Secondary inputs were PharmaTrac and IQVIA class data and the SUSTAIN-6, SELECT and STEP trial results.

  3. Analysis

    The field data overturned the starting assumption. Only 2 of 40 pharmacies were dispensing GLP-1, so the market still had to be built, not fought over. 7 of 10 physicians named cost as the main adherence barrier. 6 of 10 preferred semaglutide in practice; the 2 who preferred tirzepatide cited weight loss.

  4. Decision

    Leave the cash-pay weight-loss buyer to tirzepatide. Position GLUMIC for T2DM patients with high cardiovascular risk who pay out of pocket. Lead with a patient-support and adherence programme at initiation and month three, and treat a validated cold chain as the brand promise. Use SELECT only for its enrolled population and lead T2DM detailing with SUSTAIN-6.

  5. Outcome

    A 37-slide brand plan and a three-year Excel commercial model, delivered within the internship programme. A public 4-page summary is linked below.

Scope and limits. This is an academic launch simulation prepared during a postgraduate summer internship. GLUMIC is an illustrative brand name, and this page makes no claim of commercial implementation. The samples (n = 40 outlets, n = 10 physicians) are directional, not statistically confirmatory. Financial detail and participant identities stay in the internal annexure.

Case 02MBA analysisPublic transaction · Apr 2026

Sun Pharma × Organon: is a 103% premium defensible?

  1. Challenge

    In April 2026 Sun Pharma agreed to acquire Organon for $14.00 a share, an enterprise value of about $11.75B and the largest deal ever struck by an Indian drugmaker. The price was more than double Organon's unaffected early-April share price. The question: what is Sun actually buying, and at what risk?

  2. Evidence

    Organon's 8-K and CY2025 results, Sun Pharma's announcement, and published rating-agency and broker notes, all as available in April–May 2026.

  3. Analysis

    About 6.2x trailing EV/EBITDA on Organon's CY2025 adjusted EBITDA of $1.9B, with pro-forma net debt to EBITDA of about 2.3x at close. I assessed four strategic pillars: a top-three women's health position, immediate biosimilar scale, a cash-generating established-brands portfolio and an existing China platform. I also weighed them against Nexplanon's loss-of-exclusivity risk and Organon's internal-control weaknesses.

  4. Decision (my view)

    This is a distressed-asset purchase at a conservative multiple, made possible by Organon's governance and debt problems. It creates value only if three things go right: deleveraging, Nexplanon lifecycle management and remediation of financial controls.

  5. Outcome

    An 8-page analysis with brand-level portfolio tables and a regulatory-approval risk map. Broker estimates, such as HDFC Securities' ~27% PAT accretion by FY2028, are cited as third-party estimates, not as my modelling.

Scope and limits. MBA coursework on a public transaction, using figures as reported at the time of writing. The deal was expected to close in early 2027, subject to approvals, so its status may have changed since April 2026.

Case 03Independent conceptProduct strategy · Mar 2026

PharmaIntel India: drug information without the connectivity tax

  1. Challenge

    Most drug-information tools in India are English-only and need a constant connection. Pharmacists, medical representatives and frontline health workers in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities often have neither the language nor the signal those tools assume.

  2. Evidence

    India's language diversity, uneven connectivity and dispensing realities. Regulatory frame: CDSCO schedules, NLEM 2022 price-controlled medicines and the DPDP Act 2023.

  3. Analysis

    I defined six user segments, from retail and hospital pharmacists to medical representatives, students, consumers and brand managers, and mapped each to the features it needs. Then I made the platform choices: one codebase across web, mobile and desktop; a locally stored medicine database; on-device search.

  4. Decision

    Offline-first design in 13 Indian languages. Prioritise interaction checks, generic substitutes and NLEM price visibility at the point of dispensing, with schedule classification shown clearly.

  5. Outcome

    An 11-page product strategy and design document (version 2.0) with a phased delivery roadmap.

Scope and limits. This is an independent product concept. Performance targets in the document, such as search speed and database size, are design goals, not measured results. No released app is claimed.
